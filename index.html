8 days in Central Europe: Budapest, Vienna and the Salzkammergut lakes

This site is the day-by-day plan of a real trip: four friends, eight days, two capitals and the lakes of the Salzkammergut in Austria. An AI agent wrote the plan and kept it up to date on the road. The site is also a working example of tripwright, the open-source trip planner built from this way of planning.

How this plan was made, and what tripwright is

The plan was not written by hand. The traveler who planned the trip told an AI agent what the group wanted, in chat messages (many of them dictated by voice). The agent kept the plan, the bookings and every decision in a private trip repository. It checked trains and buses in open timetable data, checked the weather forecast, and rebuilt this site after each change. When the weather or a bus changed the day, the agent re-planned and the pages changed with it.

tripwright is the open-source version of this way of planning. It is a service that AI assistants such as Claude, ChatGPT or Codex connect to over MCP (the Model Context Protocol). In every answer it brings back your preferences and earlier decisions, it looks up transport and says what the data does not cover, and it records why the plan changed. Each person runs their own copy; there is no public service.
